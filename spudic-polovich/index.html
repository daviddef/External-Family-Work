The contradiction, and why it matters

The 1891 baptismal entry says the father was Vid(o). The 1957 death record, filled in by a son, says Stephen. The baptismal entry is the earlier and firsthand record, so Vid is the name to search first. This page does not claim that Stephen is wrong. Three explanations fit, and none is confirmed: (a) the informant had the name wrong; (b) Stephen is Janko’s grandfather or another relative and the name slipped a generation; (c) a patronymic: Vid Stjepanov, Vid son of Stjepan. Local surname lists in this parish carry house-names built exactly that way (Spudić Grgin, Markov, Matkov). Searching Croatian registers for a “Stephen Spudić” with a wife Dora would very likely find nothing, and the absence would mean nothing.

A couple that fits, found 17 October and still only a candidate. FamilySearch’s Family Tree has a Vid Spudić, born 15 November 1846 at Leskovac, son of Ivan Spudić and Ana Maglić, who married Dorotea Polović (born 4 June 1846 at Novaki, daughter of Josip Polović and Barbara Jurčević) at Leskovac on 27 November 1867. Dora is the usual short form of Dorotea. The tree entries have no sources attached and list no children.

The part that can be checked has been. The parish’s own baptism register for 1846 (image 78 and image 81 of the FamilySearch volume; a free FamilySearch sign-in is needed to view images) contains both babies with the parents’ names the tree gives: on 4 June 1846 “Dorothea”, daughter of Josephus Polovich of Novaki and Barbara née Jurcevich (the surname reading is partly uncertain); and in mid-November 1846 “Vitus”, son of Joannes Spudich of Leskovac, house number read as 16, and Anna née Maglich. So Vid and Dorotea existed, and “Vid Spudić and Dora Polović of Leskovac” is no longer a bare guess.

Not shown: that they are Janko’s parents. Their marriage and any children fall after 1857, in books that are not online. Two things to test: Vid’s family lived at house 16 in 1846, while Janko’s baptism says house 10 in 1891 (families move and houses divide); and Vid was 44 and Dorotea 45 when Janko was born, which is possible but late. Neither rules the pair out, and neither proves them. Another Vid Spudić, born 8 January 1828 in the same parish, appears in other people’s trees and is a different man.

Who worked on this branch of the tree, and why it matters. The entries for Vid, Dorotea, Ivan and Dorotea’s father were edited in 2012 by a FamilySearch contributor shown as “Debra Papa” (the first record of each person is attributed to FamilySearch itself) and carry notes of August 2013 attributed to her: “Leskovac #16” (Vid), “Novaki #13” (Dorotea and her father), “Josip was 22 when he married Barbara Jurcevic”, and “Ivan = Joannes Bapt.” Two of those notes match what the register images show (house 16 for Vid’s family; age 22 for Josip at his marriage), and the marriage dates of Josip and Barbara (26 November 1832) and of a Susana Spudić, daughter of an older Vid Spudić (26 November 1836, a different Spudić household), match the marriage book. So whoever worked on this branch was evidently reading the registers themselves, which makes it more trustworthy than most. It still has no source attached, and the contributor’s data for the years after 1857 (the 1867 marriage) cannot be checked from here. She is the single most useful person to ask: see the third message under “Draft enquiries”.

Where the siblings’ names will actually be found

The parish and civil registers at Leskovac

Janko’s siblings were presumably born in the same house, Leskovac 10, between roughly 1875 and 1905. Their baptisms were entered in the same parish baptism registers as Janko’s, in the volumes around page 184 of whichever volume he is in. Two rules govern how to get at them:

  • FamilySearch will not have them. The Record Atlas lists one FamilySearch volume for the parish: births 1832–1857, marriages 1832–1857, deaths 1782–1857 (waypoint 9R2M-YW1). That is a generation before Janko. It matters only for the parents’ side: a Vid born 1832 or later would have his own baptism there, and so would his brothers and sisters. It cannot show Janko’s siblings, because they were born after it ends.
  • After 1860 the books went to the registry office. The 1946 State Registers Act sent registers from 1860 onward to the civil registry offices (matični uredi); only entries up to 1860 went to the state archives. The catalogue of the Croatian State Archives (HDA) lists Leskovac Barilovićki only for 1782–1857. A nil in the archive catalogue for 1891 therefore says nothing about whether the book survives. It is at the registry office or the parish.
  • The 1931 certificate proves the parish itself still held the 1891 book in 1931, signed by its priest. Both parish and registry office are worth asking, which is what the two replies in the thread were saying.
  • Leskovac lay in the Military Frontier (Vojna krajina) until its dissolution in 1881; after that its records file like any Croatia-Slavonia parish. That affects the pre-1881 generation only.
Not established

I could not find a published address, email or phone number for the Leskovac parish office or for the registry office that covers Barilović, and have not invented one. The two enquiries below say who to ask and what to ask for; the requester or a Croatian-speaking relative will need to look up the current contact through the diocese’s parish directory or the municipality of Barilović.

The surname locally

Spudić is overwhelmingly a Leskovac Barilovićki name: the Croatian surname survey on Acta Croatica reports about 420 bearers in Croatia, 60 of them in Leskovac Barilovićki itself, and says that in Leskovac, Kolenovac and Vinski Vrh near Duga Resa roughly every third inhabitant is a Spudić. Polović is also a Leskovac name, 14th in the village’s present-day list. With a husband and a wife both from the same village, “Vid Spudić and Dora Polović” should be a very findable couple once the right register is open. The difficulty is access, not ambiguity.

What would turn this “maybe” into a finding

Four things, in order of strength. (1) Janko’s own baptismal entry in the 1891 register, if it gives the parents’ names in full and ties them to Vid Spudić’s 1846 family; the certificate copy already says Vid and Dora Polović, but does not name the grandparents. (2) The 1867 marriage entry of Vid and Dorotea and the baptisms of their children, 1868 to 1891, which sit in post-1857 books that are not online (the letters below ask for them). (3) A house-number trail: Vid’s parents were at house 16 in 1846 and Janko was born at house 10 in 1891, so a record showing Vid at house 10 would close that gap. (4) Hearing from the contributor who built the tree branch, who may have seen the post-1857 books. The tree is not wrong to suggest the pair; it only has no source behind it yet.

What would move this forward

Ranked by how directly each step reaches the four names. The first two cost nothing to start.

  1. Share Janko’s obituary text. The requester has it. Its exact wording (“preceded in death by…”, any named towns, any surviving relatives) tells us whether the four were in America or in Croatia, and whether the Springfield notice (26 Nov 1957, Daily Illinois State Journal) says more than the index.
  2. Write to the Leskovac parish and the registry office (drafts below). With the Mount Olive stones ruled out, the siblings’ trail most likely runs through Leskovac itself, and “siblings” may include children who died young. Ask for baptisms and deaths, house 10, 1875–1910, and for the 1867 marriage of Vid Spudić and Dorotea Polović (candidate couple above); the draft now asks for both.
  3. Errand: request Janko’s marriage certificate from the Macoupin County Clerk in Carlinville (about 1920–22; draft letter below). It names Anna’s maiden name and may name witnesses and parents, and it tests whether Anna was Tom Spudic’s daughter. No free online index reaches 1920: the ILGenWeb lists stop at 1900 and the clerk’s own online index at 1916, so this has to be a direct request. The clerk charges $5.00 for a genealogy copy of a certificate more than 50 years old.
  4. Request Petition no. 2878. Janko’s naturalization file at the Macoupin County Circuit Clerk (Carlinville) or the National Archives may name the ship, the date, the wife and the children. It is a formal application the requester has to make themselves.
  5. Read the 1913 manifest image (FamilySearch image 643 of 831 in that film group, via the index record above; April 1913, President Lincoln). Two columns matter: the nearest relative at home, and the person he was going to join with that person’s address. If the name there is “Marko” and the address is Mount Olive or Cahokia, we learn whether the “brother” is real. It needs a person to read it at full size. Tried on 17 October: the right sheet and the “Spudic” line (line 25, age 24) were found, but the relative and destination columns could not be lined up with that line at the viewer’s resolution, and what was legible nearby belongs to other passengers.
  6. Ask Eric Spudic and Karen Curlovic to compare their Ancestry trees against the baptismal certificate and the 1930 death certificate of Marco Spudich. The certificates are better sources than a tree. Draft message below.
  7. Errand: ask three tree owners what they hold. The contributor who edited and annotated the Vid and Dorotea entries on FamilySearch (the best lead), the owner of the Curlovic family tree on MyHeritage, and the Geni manager of the Polović family of Žabljak may have sourced baptisms for Vid, Dorotea or Janko that the public pages do not show (drafts below). All three are routine messages through the site’s own contact feature; no personal addresses are needed.
  8. The parish’s FamilySearch volume, 1782–1857 (Record Atlas listing), to find Vid’s own birth and his brothers and sisters. Partly done on 17 October: Vid’s own baptism (mid-November 1846) and Dorotea Polović’s (4 June 1846) are in it and have been read (see the candidate couple). Still to read: Dorotea’s parents’ marriage of 26 November 1832, the 1850–57 marriages, and the deaths. This volume cannot name Janko’s four siblings, who were born after 1857; it can only make the Vid and Dorotea identification stronger or weaker.
